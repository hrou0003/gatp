# Lecture 25: Viewing Manifolds from Atlases — Problems

1. Let S¹ = {(x, y) ∈ ℝ² | x² + y² = 1} with the subset topology from ℝ². Construct an atlas for S¹ using two charts (stereographic projection from the north and south poles). Compute the transition map and verify it is a homeomorphism.

2. Let (M, 𝒪) be a d-dimensional manifold and let (U, x) be a chart. Show that the component functions x¹, …, xᵈ: U → ℝ are continuous with respect to the subset topology on U.

3. Let A = {(Uα, xα) | α ∈ A} be an atlas for a manifold M. Show that any two charts in A are C⁰-compatible.

4. Consider (ℝ, 𝒪_std) and the atlas A = {(ℝ, id_ℝ)}. Show that A is not maximal by exhibiting a C⁰-compatible chart not in A.

5. Let M = S² = {(x, y, z) ∈ ℝ³ | x² + y² + z² = 1}. Construct an atlas with at least two charts and compute one transition map explicitly.

6. Let γ: ℝ → M be a curve on a manifold M and let (U, x) be a chart with γ(0) ∈ U. Show that γ is continuous at 0 if and only if each component function xⁱ ∘ γ is continuous at 0.

7. Let (U, x) and (V, y) be two C⁰-compatible charts on M with U ∩ V ≠ ∅. Let γ: ℝ → M be a curve with image contained in U ∩ V. Express y ∘ γ in terms of x ∘ γ and the transition map.

8. Prove that every atlas is contained in a unique maximal atlas.

9. Let (M, 𝒪) be a d-dimensional manifold. Show that for any p ∈ M, there exists a chart (U, x) with p ∈ U such that x(p) = 0 ∈ ℝᵈ.

10. Let M and N be topological manifolds of dimensions m and n respectively. Construct an atlas for M × N from atlases for M and N. What are the transition maps?
